# Pitch deck outline (≤ 10 min) and demo video script (≤ 5 min)

Red-team numbers are from the final run `docs/evidence/redteam/20261019-210204/summary.md`. Use only numbers from real runs.

## Deck: 10 slides, about 1 minute each

1. **Hufu 虎符: a tiger tally for your AI.** Team, Topic F (Agent Wallet), one line: *an AI wallet that pays on its own within what you signed, and can't be talked into the rest.*
2. **The problem, personally.** "I'm a Bangladeshi student in Melbourne. Rent, tuition, money from home: every month is a stack of transfers, fees and scam emails." AI agents can now move money, but LLMs cannot tell instructions from data. A phishing email becomes a payment order. Caps don't help when the scammer asks for less than the cap.
3. **2,200 years ago, China solved delegated authority.** Photo of the Du Tiger Tally (杜虎符). The commander acts alone for small moves; over 50 soldiers needs the ruler's half; beacon-fire emergencies need no matching. → Hufu's four tiers (T0 read, T1 act within limits, T2 student approves, T3 impossible). *Freezing never needs permission; spending does.*
4. **Ayaan's month.** Persona in Hong Kong: rent 4,500 HKD, tuition 9,000 HKD a month, phone plan, roommate split, family support from Dhaka. What the agent does: pays, converts, sets up recurring payments, compares remittance routes, catches scams.
5. **Three independent checks.** Architecture diagram: guard (off-chain) → vault (on-chain) → student, whose half is a passkey on their own phone, checked by the vault on-chain. The model never holds a key and is never trusted. Even a fully jailbroken model cannot spend beyond the caps, and only to payees the student trusts.
6. **How the guard decides.** ALLOW / ASK / BLOCK with plain-language reasons. Provenance: an address seen only in an email cannot authorise a payment. Scanner: injection, urgency, lookalike addresses. Verifier: every number and hash in a reply must come from a real tool result. Hash-chained audit, anchored on-chain.
7. **Demo moments (screenshots or live).** (0) Autopilot: it paid the phone bill on its own and flagged two scam emails before Ayaan asked; then a "stolen" agent key pays the landlord behind Hufu's back and the tripwire freezes the wallet on the next run. (a) Fake fees office email → BLOCK, explained. (b) "Mr Chan's new wallet": *the AI was fooled by the lookalike; the guard wasn't* (show both addresses and the differing middle). (c) "Send it anyway": jailbroken agent → **reverted transaction on Sepolia Etherscan**, 0 moved. (d) Approve with passkey: the dialog shows exactly what will be signed, a fingerprint signs it, the vault verifies it on-chain; lock the owner key, and a stolen key file is refused (`NotOwner`, on Sepolia).
8. **Measured, not claimed.** Red-team table: 40 attacks in 8 categories and 15 normal tasks against 4 configurations, judged on-chain. Attack success: A naive 5/40 (12%) · B guard only 1/40 · C vault only 1/40 · **D Hufu 0/40**; normal tasks completed 15/15 in D. The point to land: *each layer alone missed one attack the other caught* (guard only: split payments past a limit; vault only: an invented late fee to a saved payee). Honest finding: the red team caught two real bugs (split payments passed the per-payee limit; the model invented an amount for a saved payee), and we fixed both, on-chain and in the guard. On statistics: one run of 40 is a small sample, so we repeated A and D twice more; over 120 attempts each, A let 16 through and full Hufu none (p = 0.00002). Still say the guarantee is the vault, not the model.
9. **Compliant by design.** The NFRA's 2026 AI guidance treats AI in financial transactions as high-risk, needing human oversight, an emergency stop and a manual fallback. Hufu has all three: the student's half, freeze-anytime, and an approval queue. Sanctions screening blocks a listed address even if the student insists. Transfers of HK$8,000 or more get a travel-rule-ready record. Huitong never routes crypto where it is not permitted (Bangladesh, mainland China). Payments only, never trading. Local model: nothing personal leaves the device. One slide: the rule-to-control table from COMPLIANCE.md.
10. **What's next.** The student's phone as a trusted display for what the passkey signs; a FISCO BCOS consortium of institutions (Hufu already runs on a one-node FISCO BCOS chain); federated scam-address intelligence across banks (FATE); licensed partners' live quotes in Huitong. Close: *"Give your AI a tiger tally, not your bank account."*
